About Me

From a small town in Norway to Babson College

I grew up in Norway and came to the United States to play Division I soccer and study business — a decision that reshaped the trajectory of my education, my athletics, and honestly, how I think about discipline and long-term goals. Four years later, I graduated summa cum laude from Saint Joseph's University with a double major in Business Intelligence & Analytics and Finance, and I'm now pursuing an MS in Business Analytics at Babson College.

Academically, I'm drawn to the intersection of data, finance, and decision-making — the kind of work where a clean dashboard or a well-built model actually changes what someone decides to do. That curiosity has taken me through audit and assurance work, data analytics consulting, and peer tutoring, and it's what I'm looking to build a career around in business analytics, finance, consulting, or technology.

Outside of the classroom, I've spent most of my life training for something — soccer first, and now HYROX and distance running. I like the honesty of endurance sports: the clock doesn't care about excuses, and the only way through is consistency. That same mindset is what led me to found Thalk, an iOS app built around walking, reflection, and living a little more intentionally.

When I'm not working on a model or training, I'm usually reading philosophy or literature, listening to music, or just walking outside without my phone. I care about doing ambitious work without losing sight of why I'm doing it — that balance is probably the closest thing I have to a personal philosophy.

Beyond the Résumé

A Few Other Things

I spend a lot of my free time reading — mostly philosophy and literature, usually with a notebook nearby. I like ideas that hold up outside of a classroom: Stoicism, existentialism, and anything that forces me to ask whether I'm actually living according to what I claim to value.

Music and long, phone-free walks are how I think through problems, and writing is how I make sense of them afterward. Thalk grew directly out of that habit — I wanted to build something that made that kind of reflection more accessible to other people, not just something I did for myself.

I try to hold two things at once: genuine ambition in my professional work, and an insistence on not letting that ambition flatten everything else about how I live. Nature, physical training, and unstructured time matter to me as much as a good internship or a clean analysis.
